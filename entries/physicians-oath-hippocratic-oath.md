# The Physician's Oath — A Night Watch Anchor

**NanaDex entry · 2026-10-11 · Ethics / Night Watch**

> Doctor first. Patients first. Do no harm.

**Important:** The sentence above is a personal shorthand from conversation, **not** a verbatim quotation from the Hippocratic Oath.

## Read the source, not the memory

- **Modern Hippocratic Oath (Louis Lasagna, 1964), complete text:** [PBS NOVA — The Hippocratic Oath: Modern Version](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/doctors/oath_modern.html)
- **Classical Hippocratic Oath, Michael North translation (2002):** [U.S. National Library of Medicine — Greek Medicine](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/hmd/greek/greek_rationality.html)
- **Historical comparison and explanation:** [The Physician's Oath: Historical Perspectives (2017)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5755201/)

The modern oath begins: “I swear to fulfill, to the best of my ability and judgment, this covenant:” (Louis Lasagna, 1964; read full text at PBS above.)

## Principles to revisit on difficult shifts (paraphrases, not oath quotations)

1. Treat the patient as a human being, not a diagnosis or census number.
2. Respect scientific knowledge, and admit uncertainty.
3. Ask colleagues for help when a patient's recovery requires their skills.
4. Avoid both unnecessary treatment and failure to treat.
5. Protect patient privacy.
6. Approach decisions involving life and death with humility.
7. Remember that clinicians are human and must recognize their own limitations.

## Source accuracy

“First, do no harm” is a famous medical-ethics maxim, but **does not appear verbatim in the classical Hippocratic Oath**. Modern schools may use different oaths; this entry does not establish the exact oath the reader personally recited at graduation.

## Night Watch note

When carrying two coverage roles, clarify the backup chain and escalate if simultaneous obligations create a patient-safety gap. Seeking backup is consistent with the oath's emphasis on professional judgment and collegial help.

**Keepsake:** The oath is not a command to be invulnerable. It is a commitment to act carefully, humbly, and in patients' interests.
